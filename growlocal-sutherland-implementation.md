## 12. A2 wall planner — new deliverable

**Approved. Build as a print-ready artefact.**

**Format:** A2 portrait, 420 × 594mm, 3mm bleed, CMYK, print-ready PDF. Designed for a standard office wall.

**Design:** same system as everything else. Bottle green fascia band at the top carrying the Grow Local wordmark and gold rule. Bone body. Gold Leaf hairline rules for the grid. It must look like something an owner is happy to have on their wall — restrained, useful, not an advert with a calendar attached.

**Content:**
- Twelve-month grid for the year ahead, week numbers included.
- **UK bank holidays** marked, with England/Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland distinguished where they differ.
- **Key business dates** marked in Gold Leaf: Self Assessment deadlines (31 Jan, 31 Jul), VAT quarter ends, corporation tax reference points, P60 and P11D deadlines, payroll year end (5 Apr).
- A narrow footer band, Bottle green, carrying: the wordmark, `growlocalbusiness.co.uk`, the phone number, and one QR code roughly 25mm.
- **One line only** of promotional copy, in the footer band: *"Six free tools for local business. Scan any time."*

**Rules:**
- The planner must be genuinely useful first. Advertising occupies the footer band and nothing else.
- Verify every date against an authoritative source before finalising. **A planner with a wrong deadline on it is worse than no planner at all** — it would undermine the exact competence the campaign is selling. If any date cannot be verified confidently, omit it rather than guess.
- Deliver a version dated for the current year and structure the file so next year's is a data change, not a redesign.

## 13. Longer copy is permitted

A standing note that overrides any instinct toward brevity: on the product pages, the strategy guides and the flyer back, **longer copy is acceptable and often better**. The preference for short copy is a designer's convention, not a reader's. An owner who is genuinely interested will read a great deal.

Do not compress the "Where to use it," "Common mistakes" or "What to expect" sections for visual tidiness. Set them well, break them up with the utility face and Gold Leaf rules, and let them run.

---

## 14. What not to add

- **No urgency or scarcity tactics.** No countdowns, no "limited spots," no "offer ends." The entire campaign is positioned as the opposite of pushy, and a single scarcity device would undermine it.
- **No fake social proof.** No invented testimonials, no AI-generated faces, no placeholder review counts.
- **No pricing anywhere on the site.**
- **No cookie banner** unless analytics genuinely require one.
