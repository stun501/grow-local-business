Where to use it

Five places this earns its keep.

Most people put it in one spot and forget it. These are the placements that actually bring work in, roughly in order of what they're worth.

  1. Your Google Business Profile

    It's where most people find you, and most profiles have nothing to click.

    1. Sign in to your Google Business Profile and open Edit profile.
    2. Paste your link into Appointment link (or Website, if that's empty).
    3. Add a post saying "Get an instant price" with the same link. Posts run for seven days, so repeat monthly.

  2. Your website

    A visitor who wanted a price and found none is a visitor you've lost.

    1. Add a homepage button reading Get an instant price.
    2. Put the link in your footer so it's on every page.

  3. Mid-call, by text

    Someone ringing for a price is the warmest lead of your day.

    1. Save the link as a text shortcut on your phone.
    2. Send it while you're still talking, for anything complicated.
    3. They get something in writing, and you get their number automatically.

  4. The counter and the window

    Catches people who walk past when you're shut.

    1. Print at A5 for the window, at eye height.
    2. Print smaller for the counter.
    3. One line above it; "Scan for an instant price."

  5. Invoices and vehicles

    Past customers are the cheapest work you'll ever win.

    1. Add the QR to your invoice footer.
    2. Put it on the van. People photograph vans at traffic lights.

Common mistakes

Hiding prices completely. The worry is a competitor seeing them, or being held to a number. A range answers both. "£149–£219" is enough to start a conversation without committing you to anything.

Ranges set too narrow. Precise-looking, but it backs you into a corner. Roughly 40% between low and high is honest and still useful.

One placement, then forgotten. The businesses getting real numbers from this have it in four or five places. Twenty minutes, once.

What to expect

This won't transform your business overnight, and anyone saying otherwise is selling something.

What it does is stop a specific leak. Most businesses that put it in a few places pick up a handful of enquiries a month they'd never have known about, usually the after-hours ones. Whether that's worth it depends on what an average job is worth to you.

It costs nothing and takes a minute, so the maths is usually straightforward.
