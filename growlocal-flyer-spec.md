# Grow Local — Flyer Copy & Layout Specification

**For the AI agent. This is the definitive flyer spec. Where it conflicts with `growlocal-sutherland-implementation.md`, this document wins — see §6 for the specific resolutions. Design rules unchanged: Bottle green `#12291F`, Bone `#F2EFE6`, Gold Leaf `#C6A15B`, Deep Brass `#A8813C`, Ink `#0E1613`, Slate Green `#5C6B62`. Fraunces display, Archivo body, Archivo Narrow utility. No gradients, no stock photography, no drop shadows, no pure white.**

---

## 1. What the flyer has to do

Two jobs, and they need different solutions.

**Job one: get scanned now.** One hook, one destination, minimum decisions.

**Job two: survive not being scanned now.** The enemy is not rejection — almost nobody thinks "no." They think "later," and later never arrives. So roughly half the design effort goes into making the card worth keeping.

That split explains the whole structure below: **the front is single-minded, the back is reference material.**

---

## 2. The hook

### 2.1 Approved headline

> **Most local businesses are better than they look online.**
> **What does yours score out of 100?**

**Why this combination works.** The first line flatters and normalises simultaneously — it locates the problem in the shopfront rather than the business, which is a grievance these owners already feel and rarely hear named. It also pre-empts the shame that a bare deficit statistic would trigger, and shame produces avoidance rather than action.

The second line then does the work the first can't: it introduces a **unit** (out of 100), opens a **curiosity gap about the reader themselves**, and points at a specific object. The most interesting subject to any person is themselves, and a question about them that they cannot answer creates tension that survives them putting the card down.

Flattery alone would be pleasant and inert. The score alone would be sharp but slightly accusatory. Together, the first line earns permission for the second.

**Set both lines in the display serif**, the first at full headline size, the second at roughly 80% — same block, clear hierarchy, read as one thought.

### 2.2 Compressed variant, if the layout demands it

> **You're better than you look online. What do you score out of 100?**

Shorter and punchier, but carries a real risk: "you're better than you look" can land as backhanded, where "most local businesses are..." distributes the observation and removes the sting. **Use the approved version unless space genuinely forces the change.**

### 2.3 Sub-line

> A free tool that scores your Google listing and shows you the three things worth fixing first. Thirty seconds. Nothing to fill in.

*Note the deliberate softening from earlier drafts: "the three things worth fixing first" rather than "the three things quietly costing you customers." The headline has already established a generous frame — the sub-line must not undo it by reverting to deficit language.*

## 3. FRONT — full layout

**Band 1 — Fascia** *(top, full bleed, Bottle green, Gold Leaf hairline rules top and bottom)*

Wordmark in Bone with the Gold Leaf underline rule, centred.

Beneath, utility face, small caps, Gold Leaf:
> `A GROWTH AGENCY FOR LOCAL BUSINESS`

---

**Band 2 — The hook** *(Bone field, left-aligned, generous left margin)*

Display serif, Ink:
> **Most local businesses are better than they look online.**
> **What does yours score out of 100?**

Body face, Slate Green, beneath:
> A free tool that scores your Google listing and shows you the three things worth fixing first. Thirty seconds. Nothing to fill in.

---

**Band 3 — The certainty cluster** *(same Bone field, Gold Leaf middot markers)*

> · Nothing to install. Nothing to cancel.
> · You don't have to give us anything to see your score.
> · No sign-up. No charge. No sales pitch.

**Do not reorder or reword these.** They are sequenced deliberately: the first removes the fear of ongoing entanglement, the second removes the fear of a data grab, the third removes the fear of pursuit. In a category where quality can't be verified before purchase, buyers optimise for risk reduction rather than expected value — everyone claims excellence, almost nobody claims safety. This cluster is the safety claim, and it replaces the previous ease-and-cost bullets entirely.

---

**Band 4 — The cost signal** *(Gold Leaf hairline rule above, body face, Ink)*

> These took months to build. We give them away because they work.

**This line is load-bearing, not decorative.** "Free" with no comparison point reads as *unvalued*. Since there is no pricing anywhere in the brand, this anchors on cost of production instead — which converts free from a possible red flag into evidence of confidence.

---

**Band 5 — The action** *(QR panel)*

QR in Ink on Bone, inside a fine Gold Leaf keyline box. **No rounded corners, no shadow. Minimum 32mm square.**

Action line beside or beneath, weighted properly — not caption-sized. First two words Gold Leaf, remainder Ink:
> **SCAN NOW** — takes less time than reading this card

**Band 6 — Contact** *(bottom, full bleed, Bottle green)*

Utility face, Gold Leaf, small caps:
> `GROW LOCAL`

## 4. BACK — full layout

**Head band** *(narrow, Bottle green)*

Utility face, Gold Leaf small caps:
> `WHAT'S INSIDE`

---

**The menu** *(Bone field. Gold Leaf numeral in the utility face, tool name in the display serif, one short benefit line in the body face. Gold Leaf hairline rules between items.)*

> `01` **Foundation Scorecard**
> How your Google listing scores out of 100, and what to fix first.

> `02` **Instant Quote Form**
> Give a price to the people who look you up at midnight and never ring.

> `03` **Google Review QR**
> Ask while they're still smiling — not three days later by email.

> `04` **Review Reply Writer**
> Answer every review properly, in your own voice, in about a minute.

> `05` **Booking Link**
> One link that ends the back-and-forth over times.

> `06` **Instant Valuation** — *for estate agents*
> Catch the sellers browsing at midnight, months before they'd call you.

**Benefit lines must be tightened to a single line each** to make room for §4's checklist. If any runs to two lines at the chosen type size, cut words rather than reducing the checklist.

---

**The downsell** *(directly beneath the menu, body face, italic or Slate Green)*

> Most businesses only ever need one of these. Take the one that fits.

**Keep this.** Talking someone out of taking more than they need costs nothing and buys a disproportionate amount of trust, because almost nobody does it. It also lowers the threshold — the reader isn't signing up for six things, just one.

---

**The useful box** *(Gold Leaf keyline panel — this is the single most important addition to the card)*

Utility face heading, Gold Leaf small caps:
> `FIVE THINGS THAT DECIDE WHETHER PEOPLE CHOOSE YOU`

Numbered list, body face, Ink:
> 1. A photo added in the last month
> 2. Your hours right, including bank holidays
> 3. Every review replied to
> 4. The right primary category
> 5. Somewhere to click when they're ready

Beneath, Slate Green, smaller:
> Most listings miss three of these.

**Why this box exists.** Every flyer in existence is about the sender; almost none is useful to the recipient before they respond. This one is. Three consequences: reciprocity fires at the moment of reading rather than after scanning, the card becomes reference material that gets pinned rather than binned (which is the direct answer to the deferral problem), and it makes the Scorecard the obvious next step without having to ask for it.

The closing line also carries the normalisation that the earlier "most score under 50" headline was doing — so nothing is lost by moving away from that as the hook.

---

**The value line** *(beneath the box)*

> One extra job a month and it's paid for itself. It's free anyway.

*Relocated from the front — see §6.*

---

**Closing block** *(Gold Leaf full-width rule above)*

Display serif, small:
> **Know someone who could use this?**

Body face:
> Pass it on. That's how most people find us.

Utility face, Gold Leaf small caps, bottom:
> `GROWLOCALBUSINESS.CO.UK`

## 5. Physical specification

**Stock:** 400gsm uncoated, matte, no lamination. Uncoated is what reads as expensive here — gloss undoes it instantly.

**Two additions, both cheap and both worth doing:**

**Painted or deep-dyed edge** in Bottle green. Materially trivial, noticed instantly in the hand, and it repositions the whole object before a word is read. This is the same move as a foil cap on a can of mineral water.

**A standing tent-card variant.** Print a short run scored to fold into a small standing card. A flat card gets filed or binned; a standing card occupies counter space and is seen daily for weeks. Same object, different physical mode, completely different lifespan. Worth testing at low volume before committing.

**Format:** A6 portrait, double-sided, 3mm bleed, crop marks, CMYK. 4-up on A4 for proofing only — final run on the heavy stock.

**Hard rule: the front must work alone.** Assume half the people who see this never turn it over.

## 7. The split test

Print **two versions of the front only**, with **different QR destination URLs** so scans can be attributed. Backs identical.

- **Arm A:** the combined Scorecard hook in §2.1, with the full front layout above.
- **Arm B:** the existing *"Six free tools for local business."* headline and sub-line, with the **new certainty cluster and cost-signal line** applied so the only variable being tested is the hook.

**What this actually tests:** whether leading with one specific tool beats leading with the collection. That's the real strategic question, and reasoning alone won't settle it — I'd put it at close to even odds, which is exactly when a test is worth running.

**Do not run three arms.** Volume at this scale won't support it, and a three-way split produces a result nobody can act on.

## 8. Standing rules — do not reintroduce

- **No urgency or scarcity devices.** No countdowns, no "limited," no "offer ends." The entire campaign is an inversion of pushy, and one scarcity device would undo it.
- **No pricing anywhere**, on the flyer or the site.
- **No "high street."** Say local business.
- **No "automation," "AI," "workflow," "consultancy" or "solution"** in customer-facing copy.
- **No stock photography and no imitation-handwriting fonts.** If the signature file isn't available, leave the space and flag it.
- **No second company name.** Grow Local only.
